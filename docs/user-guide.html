1. Where your data lives

Nothing is uploaded unless you sign in. Signed out, every record stays in this browser, on this device, and is read straight from it.

Sign in with Google and your records are kept in your own private area of the app's database, so a second device signed in as you picks them up. Even then, a copy stays on the device, so an open app keeps working if the connection drops.

Archived files — salary sheets and bill documents you attach — never leave the device that holds them. They travel only inside a JSON backup.

The one habit worth keeping: export a JSON backup now and then, whichever mode you use. It is the only copy that contains everything, and it is yours.

2. Getting started

The first time the app opens it offers four ways in:

  1. Sign in with Google — start an account that syncs between devices.
  2. Start Blank — an empty set of records, private to this device.
  3. Load Demo Data — invented figures, to see how the app behaves before committing anything real.
  4. Import Backup — restore a .json backup you exported before.

Demo data can be replaced at any time from Backup & Import, and Clear All Data empties everything and starts again.

The usual first month

  1. Open Salary & Savings and choose Add Payslip. Enter the month's basic pay, allowance, transportation and the deductions from your payslip.
  2. Leave Income Tax blank if you do not have the figure to hand — the app estimates it — and enter the real number when you do.
  3. Log overtime days under Overtime. The total flows into that month's payslip automatically.
  4. Add what you spent under Expenditures. Savings for the month fall as you add them.
  5. Open Dashboard to see the year so far.

3. Installing it as an app

Finance Tracker can be installed from the browser. It then has its own icon and its own window, with no tabs or address bar around it, and opens like any other app.

What installing changes — and what it does not

  • You get an icon and a standalone window. Nothing else about the app differs.
  • The records are the same records. The installed app uses the same browser profile underneath, so it and the tab show the same data on that device.
  • It still needs a connection to start. The app is not bundled for offline launch, so opening it with no network shows the browser's error page. Once it is open, your records are read from the device.
  • Uninstalling removes the icon, not your records. They stay in the browser profile until you clear the site's data — so export a backup before removing anything.

Prefer a real desktop app? Finance Records is also packaged as a macOS application. It keeps its records in a file on your Mac rather than in a browser profile, and stores attached salary sheets and bill documents on disk. Backups written by either app open in the other.

4. Finding your way around

The menu down the left side groups the sections: Salary (Salary & Savings, Overtime), Stocks (Stock Revenue, Daily Records) and Other (Expenditures, Debt Records, Backup & Import, Help). On a phone the menu slides in from the Menu button.

Search

The search box at the top searches your records and the Help page at once. Results are grouped by section; pick one and the app opens that section with the matching row highlighted and the right year already selected. Month names, formatted amounts and plain words all work.

Ocean and Dark

The switch beside the search box changes the look between the light Ocean theme and Dark. Your choice is remembered on this device and is not part of your records or backups.

Colour, everywhere

Green means money gained or on track. Red means money lost, owed, or falling short. On the dashboard charts, falling short means take-home under half of gross pay, or a stock total below its target.

Salary and stock are kept apart. Salary & Savings and Overtime are the salary side; Daily Records and Stock Revenue are the stock side. Stock movements are never netted off your pay, so a heavy trading month cannot make your salary savings look bad.

5. The dashboard

Four figures across the top, then two charts. Every figure is a button — click it to open the section it came from.

TileWhat it shows
Salary · IncomeGross pay for the year so far, with take-home beneath it. Months still to come appear only in the projection line.
SavingsTake-home minus recorded expenditures, for months that have already happened. The caption shows both parts.
Stock · Win TotalThe latest actual cumulative win that year. The tile moves from red through amber to green by how far it sits below or above that month's target, and the caption states the gap in yen.
Outstanding DebtEvery debt amount added together, with a count of records and distinct lenders.

The charts

  • Salary — gross income in blue and take-home in green, month by month, with the gap between them shaded. Take-home under half of gross turns red.
  • Stock — the target line against your actual cumulative, with a marker each month: ▲ when you are at or above target, ▼ when below.
  • Months still to come are dashed, with hollow markers. On the salary chart they are the projected months the headline totals leave out.

Hover and zoom

Hover or tap a month to read its figures. Scroll or pinch to zoom into a few months — on a narrow window hold Ctrl or ⌘ while scrolling — drag sideways to move along the year, and double-click or press Reset to see the whole year again. Zoomed in, the axis refits to the months in view.

6. Recording a month

Salary & Savings holds one row per month. The first columns are the money story — gross, take-home, expenditures, savings goal, actual savings, cumulative capital — and the payslip that produced them follows in the same row. Year and month stay in place while you scroll across the payslip columns.

Two kinds of month

  • With a payslip. Press Payslip on the row, or Add Payslip in the toolbar. Gross income and take-home are then calculated from what you enter, and the row's payslip columns fill in.
  • Entered by hand. Add Manual Month is for a month with no payslip — a bonus, typically — where you type gross and take-home yourself. Its payslip columns stay blank.

Bonuses

A bonus is its own row, entered as a manual month. It counts from the month it is paid: the year's first bonus from June, a later one from December. Until that month arrives the dashboard treats it as projected and keeps it out of the headline totals.

Projected months

Months that have not happened yet are shaded and tagged projected. The figures are whatever you entered ahead of time; the dashboard shows them separately rather than in its totals.

Deleting a month deletes its payslip with it. That is deliberate — the payslip would otherwise rebuild the row on the next render. The confirmation names the month so you know what is going.

7. Overtime

Log overtime a day at a time: hours, any extra hours, and the hourly rate. The amount is worked out for you.

FieldHow it is worked out
Amount(OT Hours + Extra OT) × Hourly Rate
Monthly OT SummaryAdds that month's entries and shows them against the matching payslip, so you can check a payslip before entering it.

Logged overtime wins. A month's overtime entries become that month's Overtime Pay on the payslip. The figure typed into a payslip is used only for months with no overtime entries at all.

8. Expenditures

Dated records of what you actually spent. Category / Name identifies the spending; the note is optional. Filter by year and month, and the page totals the selection and the year.

  • Each expense is deducted from take-home in its own year and month.
  • Editing, moving or deleting an expense recalculates monthly savings and cumulative capital immediately.
  • Savings can go negative when spending exceeds take-home — that is shown in red, not hidden.

Debt is separate. A debt balance is not automatically an expense. Record a payment here only when money actually left your hands, so nothing is counted twice.

9. Stock: daily records and targets

Daily Records

A day-by-day log of stock results: a gain is positive, a loss negative. It rolls up into the stock side and is never subtracted from your salary.

10. Debt records

What you owe, as you understand it: a lender or group, a label or date, the amount, and an optional note. The dashboard adds every amount exactly as entered and counts the distinct lenders behind the total.

Debts are never netted off savings or stock results. They sit alongside them, so a balance you are carrying does not quietly flatter or spoil another figure.

11. How every number is worked out

Amounts are yen throughout. Nothing here is rounded away: what you enter is what is added.

FigureFormula
Gross TotalBasic + Allowance + Overtime Pay + Transportation
Total DeductionHealth Insurance + Pension + Employment Insurance + Residence Tax + Income Tax
Take-home (Net Received)Gross Total − Total Deduction
ExpendituresThe sum of that month's records in the Expenditures section
Actual SavingsTake-home − that month's expenditures
Savings GoalWhatever you set. It is never calculated.
Cumulative CapitalA running total of actual savings through the year; every new year starts again from zero
Overtime Amount(OT Hours + Extra OT) × Hourly Rate

Gross Total, Total Deduction and Net Received are recalculated every time a payslip is saved, which is why the payslip editor shows them read-only.

The income tax estimate

Used only when Income Tax is left blank. It annualises Basic + Allowance + Overtime — transportation is excluded, as commuting allowance is not taxable — subtracts the employment-income deduction, then social insurance and the basic deduction, applies the tax brackets with the surtax, and divides by twelve.

Treat this as a rough guide — it ignores bonuses and will not match a payslip exactly. Enter the figure from your payslip whenever you have it; your own number is always kept.

12. Backups and workbooks

FileWhat it holds
Backup (.json)Everything: every record, plus archived salary sheets and bill files. This is the one to keep.
Workbook (.xlsx)The records only, one sheet per section, plus a Summary sheet of the dashboard figures. Archived files are listed but not included.

Importing replaces. Importing a backup or a workbook replaces all current records rather than merging into them. Export a backup first, every time.

Old backups still open: a file written before a feature existed comes back with that section empty, and figures recorded under earlier names are carried across to their current ones.

Starting from a spreadsheet

Export a workbook first, even with no records in it. That gives you the exact sheet names and column headings the importer expects — fill those sheets in, then import the file back. Building a spreadsheet from scratch and hoping it matches will not work.

Rows come back in as new entries, so a workbook suits setting records up or reworking them in bulk rather than merging into what is already there.

13. Signing in and syncing

Signing in is optional and reversible. Signed out, records never leave the device.

QuestionAnswer
What syncs?Salary months and their payslips, Overtime, Stock Revenue, Daily Records, Expenditures and Debt Records.
What does not?Archived salary sheets and bill files. They stay on the device that holds them and travel only in a JSON backup.
Which copy wins?Signing in pulls your account's copy onto this device. After that, each save sends what changed here, record by record. A record edited on two devices keeps whichever was saved last.
Offline?Changes are kept on the device and go up with the next save. Sync now sends everything again.
Importing while signed in?An imported backup replaces your records and is then sent to your account, so it replaces that copy on every device. Clear All Data does the same with an empty set.

Before editing on a device that has been open a while, reload it. Last save wins per record, so a stale page can overwrite something newer from another device.

14. Quick answers

A month is missing from the dashboard totals

It has not happened yet. Projected months are shaded in the table and shown separately on the dashboard. A bonus counts from June or December depending on whether it is the year's first.

The estimated income tax does not match my payslip

It will not — it annualises rather than following the monthly withholding table, and it ignores bonuses. Enter the figure from the payslip; your own number is always kept.

Some payslip fields will not let me type in them

Gross Total, Total Deduction and Net Received are calculated from the other fields and rewritten on save.

I cannot delete a row

Deleting a salary month removes its payslip too, and says so. If you want to keep the month but drop the payslip, delete the payslip from its own editor instead.

Where did my archived files go after restoring on another device?

Only a JSON backup carries them. A workbook lists them by name but not their contents, and sync never uploads them.

How do I start over?

Backup & Import → Clear All Data empties everything. Export a backup first if there is any chance you will want it back.
