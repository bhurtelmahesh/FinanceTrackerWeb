    How every number is worked out

    Amounts are yen. Green means money gained or on track, red means money lost, owed or falling short — the same rule everywhere in the app. On the dashboard charts, falling short means take-home under half of gross pay, or a stock total below its target.

    Salary and stock are kept apart. Salary Details and Overtime are the salary side. Daily Records and Stock Revenue are the stock side. Stock movements are never netted off your pay, so a heavy trading month cannot make your salary savings look bad.

    Salary Details

    Overtime Pay
    The total of your Overtime entries for that month. Logged overtime wins: the figure typed here is used only for months with no overtime entries.
    Gross Total
    Basic + Allowance + Overtime Pay + Transportation.
    Total Deduction
    Health Insurance + Pension + Employment Insurance + Residence Tax + Income Tax.
    Net Received
    Gross Total − Total Deduction.
    Income Tax
    Your own figure is kept whenever you enter one. Leave it blank and the app estimates it.

    Gross Total, Total Deduction and Net Received are always recalculated on save, so they are shown read-only.

    Income tax estimate

    Used only when Income Tax is left blank. It annualises Basic + Allowance + Overtime (Transportation is excluded, as commuting allowance is not taxable), subtracts the employment-income deduction, then social insurance and the basic deduction, applies the income tax brackets with the 2.1% surtax, and divides by twelve.

    Treat this as a rough guide. It uses the post-2025 figures throughout — a 650,000 employment-income deduction floor and a 580,000 basic deduction — but it ignores bonuses, and Japan withholds monthly tax from a published table rather than by annualising, so it will not match a payslip exactly. Enter the figure from your payslip whenever you have it.

    Daily Records

    What a number means
    This is the day-by-day stock log: a gain is positive, a loss negative. It rolls up into the Stock section and is never subtracted from your salary.
    A status instead of a number
    Type a word instead of an amount — CLOSED for a holiday, for example — and the day is stored with a zero amount and that marker.
    Zero versus empty
    A cell showing 0 is a day recorded as zero. An empty cell has no record at all. Clearing a cell deletes its record.
    Saturday and Sunday
    Shown as a red chip until you enter something. Weekends are fully editable.
    Monthly Total
    The sum of that month’s amounts. A dash means the month has no records at all.

    Monthly Savings

    Gross Income
    The month’s Gross Total — the same Total shown on the Overtime page.
    Actual Savings
    What actually reached you: Gross Income − Total Deduction. Stock activity is never netted off it.
    Savings Goal
    Whatever you set. It is never calculated.
    What counts as a month so far
    Simply whether the month has happened yet, by the calendar. A bonus counts from the month it is paid: the year’s first bonus from June, a later one from December.
    Cumulative Capital
    A running total of Actual Savings through the year: each month carries on from the one before it, and every new year starts again from zero.

    Rows generated from Salary Details cannot be deleted here — delete the matching salary detail instead. Months that have not happened yet are shaded and tagged projected: the figures are the ones you entered ahead of time, and the dashboard keeps them out of its headline totals.

    Dashboard

    Each figure is a button: click it to open the section it comes from.

    Salary · Gross Income
    Gross pay for the months that have already happened, including any bonus already paid. While any months are still to come, the caption gives the projected full-year figure — the same total with those months included.
    Salary · Take-home Saved
    The same split for what reached you after deductions, with the projected full year in the caption.
    Stock · Win Total
    The latest Actual Win Cumulative recorded that year. The caption gives that same month’s Target Cumulative, so you can see the figure against the goal it was measured on.
    Outstanding Debt
    Every Debt Amount added together, exactly as entered. The caption counts the records and the distinct lenders behind that total.
    Salary chart
    Gross income in blue and take-home in green, month by month. Take-home turns red, marked ▼, wherever it falls below half of gross pay. Each bonus has its own shaded column, placed where it was paid.
    Stock chart
    Actual Win Cumulative against Target Cumulative in gold. Actual is green, marked ▲, on or above target and red, marked ▼, below it, with the gap between the two lines shaded to match. The latest figure is labelled at the end of the line.
    Months still to come
    Dashed on both charts, with hollow markers. On the Salary chart they are the projected months that the headline totals leave out.
    Hover and zoom
    Hover or tap a month for its figures. Scroll or pinch on a chart to zoom in on a few months — on a narrow window hold Ctrl or ⌘ while scrolling — drag sideways to move along the year, and double-click or press Reset to see the whole year again. Zoomed in, the axis fits the months in view.

    Stock Revenue

    Monthly Win
    This month’s Actual Win Cumulative − the previous recorded month’s. The first month with a figure shows zero, as there is nothing to compare against.
    Vs Target
    Actual Win Cumulative − Target Cumulative.
    Result
    A tick above target, a cross below, a dash when the month has no actual figure or lands exactly on target.

    Overtime

    Amount
    (OT Hours + Extra OT) × Hourly Rate.
    Monthly OT Summary
    Adds that month’s entries and shows them against the matching salary detail, so a payslip can be checked before entering it.

    Backups and workbooks

    Backup (.json)
    Everything: every record, plus the archived salary sheets and bill files. This is the one to keep. Importing a backup replaces all current records.
    Workbook (.xlsx)
    A spreadsheet of the records only, one sheet per menu, with a Summary sheet of the dashboard figures. Archived files are listed but their contents are not included, so a workbook is not a substitute for a backup.

    Starting from a spreadsheet? Export a workbook first, even with no records in it. That gives you the exact sheet names and column headings the importer expects — fill those sheets in, then import the file back. Building a spreadsheet from scratch and hoping it matches will not work.

    Importing a workbook replaces the current records too, so export a backup before you try it. Rows come back in as new entries, so a workbook is best for setting records up or reworking them in bulk rather than for merging into what is already there.

    Your data

If you sign in with Google, financial records sync through your private Firebase account path and keep a device cache for offline use. Salary-sheet and bill files remain only on this device. Export a JSON backup regularly in either mode.
